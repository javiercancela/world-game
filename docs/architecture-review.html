Game architecture

A conversational RPG with an authoritative world engine. Language models interpret and perform; typed state, rules, and recorded events keep the adventure consistent.

Prepared 7 October 2026 Scope: current implementation + review decisions Adventure: Gate at Dusk

This document explains how the game works and what the design means for the experience you can build. The goals below are inferred from the project notes and remain yours to confirm.

01 / Intent & fit

What this design is aiming for

One player explores an authored world through free-form English. Conversation, deception, exploration, items, promises, and conflict have persistent consequences. The first adventure asks Lea to recover a ledger and carry it outside the gate, with a scheduled threat at dusk.

Models propose. The engine commits.

A generated sentence becomes a mechanical fact only through a supported, validated event. World state survives independently of model memory or a conversation transcript.

Working assumptions about your goals — each is a review point, not a confirmed requirement.

The main product decision

The current design fits a small authored adventure with flexible conversation and reliable mechanics. An open-ended sandbox would need broader world-creation policies, more action coverage, and more independent NPC behavior.

02 / System map

One process owns the game

The Python application coordinates input, model calls, rules, and persistence synchronously. Model roles are separate requests with different contexts; they share the campaign's configured provider. No autonomous agent framework is needed.

03 / World & knowledge

State is explicit, perspective is personal

Definitions

The immutable story package defines the starting world, rules, profiles, and authored possibilities. A campaign saves its exact package and compatibility identifiers.

Runtime state

The world snapshot records what has changed. Inventory lists, visible scenes, and model contexts are derived views, rebuilt from these records.

RecordMeaningExample
Entity + componentsStable ID with identity, placement, actor, item, portal, or Fate data.The key is physically inside Lea's inventory because its container is Lea.
Canonical propositionA story fact with true, false, or unknown truth and provenance.“Lea is expected at court” is false.
Knowledge & beliefWhat an actor has observed or learned, and their stance toward a proposition.Oren can believe the invitation claim while it remains false.
Conversation & offerParticipants, topic, speech, and explicit pending terms.Sen offers her key in return for a promise to return it.
Commitment & relationshipPersistent obligations, fulfillment or breach, and bounded directed trust.Breaking the key promise reduces Sen's trust in Lea.
Quest, clock & scheduleProgress, terminal result, ticks, and named due effects.The tick-20 retrieval can remove a ledger still in the records room.

Placement is the single source for possession; inventory is computed from it. Portals connect locations through endpoints. Validation rejects cycles, missing references, invalid resources, and inconsistent grounding.

Information is filtered before a model sees it

The interpreter receives the player's view. An NPC receives its own observations, beliefs, profile, and heard speech. The narrator receives the player's committed view and permitted event references. Another actor's private records are excluded from these projections.

This supports deception and discovery: saying something does not make it true, a listener can be mistaken, and a player's guess does not automatically become character knowledge. The human transcript is separate from explicit in-world knowledge.

Reliability limit

Filtering and structured disclosure checks reduce leaks. Free-form prose can still imply an unsupported fact; semantic consistency needs live evaluation. The engine does not promote that prose into canonical state.

04 / A turn, end to end

Resolve an attempt before describing it

  1. Receive or resume

    The CLI checks for pending work. Commands such as /look, /sheet, and /inventory read saved state without advancing time or calling a model.

  2. Interpret within the player's perspective

    The model returns an Intent or clarification. References must come from the permitted view. Ambiguity is resolved before spending gameplay resources.

  3. Check legality and lock the plan

    Code selects a registered rule and checks possession, proximity, tools, locks, and policy. The plan fixes action, skill, opposition, bonuses, costs, and available consequences.

  4. Roll and pause for decisions

    Engine-owned dice are recorded before display. Invokes, deliberate rerolls, harm allocation, and optional costs update a durable pending resolution.

  5. Stage the consequences

    Apply player effects and bounded NPC voice responses to a working state. Advance ticks, process due handlers, evaluate quests, and apply scene boundaries and observations.

  6. Validate and commit

    Validate the event batch and resulting state. One SQLite transaction stores the journal, new snapshot and RNG state, completed interaction, and factual output.

  7. Present the committed result

    The narrator may add prose around the factual outcome. Failed or rejected narration uses the saved fallback. Presentation failure cannot undo the committed action.

Worked example · actual offline fixture

“I show Oren the seal and say the court expects me. Let me in.”

Initial result: a provisional tie

Deceive 4 + Official Bearing 2 + dice −2 = effort 4, against difficulty 4. The tie would allow entry but schedule verification.

The game can be saved and reloaded here with the same dice, stakes, and choices.

Player choice: a paid +2 invoke

Lea spends one Fate point to invoke her concept aspect. Effort becomes 6; margin +2 is success. After the invoke window closes, the batch commits.

The gate opens; Oren believes the claim; Lea has 2 Fate points; tick advances to 1. The invitation remains false, and verification is not scheduled.

Technical retries preserve play. They retain recorded dice and successful calls. A purchased reroll is a separate game choice. Cancellation is available before revealed dice or accepted resource-changing choices; afterward, quitting saves the pending decision.

05 / Rules & creativity

Freedom has an authored boundary

The current ruleset is fate-condensed-cli-v1: a bounded adaptation with four Fate actions, four −1/0/+1 dice, aspects, free and paid invokes, stress, consequences, recovery, and concessions. The implementation specification replaces the original architecture's illustrative d20 rules.

Model roleCurrent authorityStatus
InterpreterProposes one supported action, speech, references, or a clarification. Cannot declare success.Implemented
NPC performerVoices an already-settled authored response from its perspective. Cannot add an offer or alter the settled response. At most two utterances are voiced per action.Implemented
NarratorDescribes a committed result, preserving factual fallback text and using permitted references. Its extra prose is not world authority.Implemented
AdjudicatorIntended to select bounded rulings for unusual attempts. Schema and prompt exist, but the current coordinator does not call this role.Extension point
Decision provider / JevA typed protocol and scripted adapter exist. No live Jev integration or live semantic-relevance route is enabled.Deferred

What can vary today

  • English phrasing, speech, and NPC voice.
  • Approach within supported methods: bluff, honest collateral, discover a concern, use a key, pick a lock, or fight.
  • Grounded advantages and supported environmental effects, such as wedging a carried object in a portal.
  • New claim records with unknown truth, when admitted through the supported path.

What remains engine-owned

  • Legal targets, reachability, possession, skill and stunt eligibility.
  • Difficulty, recorded dice, outcomes, Fate-point payments, and harm.
  • Transfers, disclosures, promises, schedules, and quest transitions.
  • Which persistent entities or effects can be created. Authored exceptions, such as a compel receipt, are explicit.

Unsupported mechanics cannot be invented just because a model describes them. The handler and effect allowlists deliberately constrain useful new objects, exits, abilities, NPCs, and mystery solutions.

Fate profile and action coverage

The profile uses nine merged skills, three passive +2 stunts, finite advantage and consequence templates, event compels, authored scene/session transitions, and nonlethal defeat. NPC invoke spending follows a deterministic policy and a shared scene GM pool.

The registered handlers cover speech, social overcome, advantage creation/discovery, movement, item presentation/taking/transfer/retrieval, keys, lockpicking, attacks, treatment, waiting, and offer acceptance. Free-form input ultimately maps to this finite surface.

These choices shape pacing and available solutions. Expanding the action registry adds real gameplay capability; changing prompts alone does not.

06 / Runtime & saves

A local core with replaceable model adapters

Stack: Python 3.12+, uv, Pydantic 2, Typer, Rich, standard-library SQLite, and the OpenAI Python SDK for both live adapters. The deterministic reducer performs no model calls, network access, clock reads, or fresh dice draws.

ProviderUseOperational boundary
ScriptedOffline play, reproducible demonstrations, and tests.No network or credentials. Understands the authored adventure vocabulary.
Local DefaultFree-form input through an OpenAI-compatible Chat Completions endpoint.Default is the existing Bonsai 2 27B server at http://127.0.0.1:8080/v1. The game connects to it; it does not start or load the model.
OpenAICloud generation through the Responses API.Requires an explicit model and API key. Provider choice does not silently change on failure.

All live outputs pass through strict wire schemas and domain validation before game admission. Defaults are a 30-second request timeout, eight total provider attempts, 120 seconds of provider work per action, and an 8,000-token conservative context allowance. Waiting for the player consumes no provider budget.

The synchronous input loop waits during generation. Successful calls are cached with interaction, role, context, prompt, schema, and provider identity. Environment settings configure the endpoint/model; a campaign preserves its chosen provider across saves.

What the campaign database retains

Each campaign has its own SQLite database, normally under ~/.local/share/world-game/campaigns/. It retains the exact story package, snapshots, committed event batches and ordered events, pending interactions, choice responses, model-call records, and presentation records.

Durable gameplay

A pending turn stores the locked plan, staged events, dice, provisional balances, current choice, and revision. Reload resumes that decision. No database write transaction remains open during player input or a model call.

Reproducible history

Offline replay applies recorded events and compares state hashes with saved snapshots. Export/import preserves the package, world, pending decisions, dialogue, and RNG continuation. Replay never needs the model to repeat its answer.

Useful architectural guarantees

Committed input and choice identities prevent duplicate application; version checks reject stale work. Saving or loading does not refresh resources. Recovery creates a new database. Portable exports contain canonical secrets and the full story, while excluding API credentials and model-call logs.

07 / Limits & next steps

Where your goals could outgrow v0.1

The world model and event path are reusable foundations. The current implementation is still closely tied to Gate at Dusk: action code references Lea, Oren, Sen, the ledger quest, and specific story outcomes. A second adventure would require refactoring as well as new data.

Potential extension directions. These are recommendations for discussion, not promised features.
If your goal is…Current limitationNext architectural work
Broad improvisationFinite methods and templates; no active model adjudicator.Add evaluated bounded adjudication, more grounded effects, and explicit policies for introducing canon.
Independent NPC livesAuthored reactions, conflict actions, and scheduled handlers; no general autonomous goal planner.Add scheduled NPC intentions with per-actor perspective and budgets, through the same event path.
Many authored storiesReusable records but adventure-specific action and quest logic.Move story-specific selectors and consequences into validated configuration or registered story handlers; prove the split with a second story.
Long campaignsA snapshot per batch; recent context is bounded; history queries scan the journal.Measure save/context growth; add indexes, retrieval, and summaries with source provenance when needed.
Faster, smoother playSynchronous generation and explicit mechanical choice windows.Measure role latency and interruptions; tune display and call policy; consider async orchestration.
Graphical or multiplayer playOne local player and CLI; no networking or shared-session protocol.Expose an application command/view boundary for a UI. Multiplayer also needs actor authority, concurrency, and synchronized choices.

A practical sequence, conditional on your review

  1. Confirm the experience: authored adventure or sandbox, how much Fate is visible, and desired NPC autonomy.
  2. Evaluate actual live play: interpretation, response time, character knowledge, narration, and unsupported requests.
  3. Prove reusable boundaries: build a small second story and remove adventure-specific coupling revealed by it.
  4. Expand the chosen dimension: richer actions, NPC planning, long-term consequences, or a new interface.

Multiplayer, procedural campaigns, character-creation UI, advancement, spell systems, general contests/challenges, vector retrieval, autonomous agent frameworks, and live Jev integration are deferred in the specification.

08 / Your review

Does this support the game you want?

Start with the experience you want, then mark the decisions that need to change. The form is a local review aid; it does not modify the game.

With JavaScript enabled, notes are saved in this browser when local storage is available. Export your review to keep a portable copy. Nothing is sent to a server.

1. Keep engine authority over the world

Models propose language and supported attempts; only validated events change gameplay. This is the strongest foundation for continuity, saves, and explainable consequences.

2. Begin with authored, bounded improvisation

Current actions and consequences are finite. Decide whether to deepen this adventure style or make broader improvisation the next priority.

3. Use the bounded Fate profile and explicit choices

Aspects and post-roll decisions give the player mechanical control, with interruptions for invokes, costs, and harm. Decide how visible and frequent those decisions should be.

4. Start with authored NPC reactions

NPC perspectives are explicit, but their main responses are selected by story mechanics. More independent agency needs planning and scheduling beyond the current voice layer.

5. Keep a local, single-player CLI first

One process and SQLite are easy to inspect and recover. The input loop waits for model generation; a graphical or shared experience introduces new requirements.

6. Prove reuse with a second small adventure

A second story would test whether the core really separates mechanics from content, before adding open-world generation or a much larger campaign.
